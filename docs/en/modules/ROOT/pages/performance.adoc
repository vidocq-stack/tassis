= Performance
:description: What Tassis has been measured to do, in the JVM and through the network, beside Apache ActiveMQ Artemis and Apache Qpid JMS; what is still slow; and how to run the benchmarks.

[.lead]
Tassis is measured with JMH, alone and beside Apache ActiveMQ Artemis and Apache Qpid JMS. In the JVM a client sends and receives 3.3 million messages a second. Through the network the server holds its place beside Artemis, and the client, which asks for one message at a time unless told to fetch ahead, is as fast as Qpid JMS once it is.

The source of this page is `BENCH.md` at the root of the repository, which holds the machine, the exact command and the raw results of each run. Every figure here comes from its entry BENCH-20261012-03, unless another is named. If the two differ, `BENCH.md` is right.

[IMPORTANT]
====
One machine, a desktop (AMD Ryzen 7 5800X, Temurin 25, Linux under WSL2), on 2026-10-12. These figures give orders of magnitude and tell a change from the previous run. They are not figures to quote against another product, and a difference of a few percent between two of them means nothing.
====

== What is measured

The benchmarks are in the module `tassis-bench`. Each is written once, against Jakarta Messaging, and run with whoever is behind it:

[cols="1,1,2"]
|===
| Name | Client | Broker

| `tassis-vm` | Tassis | Tassis, in the JVM
| `artemis-vm` | Artemis | Artemis 2.57.0, in the JVM
| `tassis-amqp` | Tassis | a `TassisServer`, AMQP 1.0 on the loopback
| `qpid-tassis` | Qpid JMS 2.11.0 | a `TassisServer`, AMQP 1.0 on the loopback
| `tassis-artemis` | Tassis | Artemis, AMQP 1.0 on the loopback
| `qpid-artemis` | Qpid JMS | Artemis, AMQP 1.0 on the loopback
|===

Messages are non-persistent, and Artemis keeps nothing on disk and checks no user, as Tassis does. Everything else is what each provider does by default. One difference remains: Tassis returns from `send` when the broker has the message, where the client of Artemis and Qpid JMS return before the broker answered.

== A message sent and received

One thread sends a message of 100 bytes to a queue and receives it, on a session that stays open.

[cols="2,1,1"]
|===
| Client and broker | Round trips a second | Allocated per round trip

| Tassis, in the JVM | 3 294 000 | 2.1 kB
| Artemis, in the JVM | 10 700 | 6.3 kB
| Tassis client, Tassis server | 2 770 | 38 kB
| Qpid JMS, Tassis server | 4 750 | 23 kB
| Tassis client, Artemis | 2 510 | 24 kB
| Qpid JMS, Artemis | 6 030 | 9.3 kB
|===

In the JVM, Tassis is three hundred times faster than Artemis. This is not a contest: Artemis reaches its broker of the JVM through its protocol and its thread pools, Tassis through a method call. It is what a broker in the JVM is for.

Through the network, read the table by its columns. With the same client, Qpid JMS, the Tassis server does 4 750 round trips a second and Artemis 6 030. With the same server, the Tassis client does 2 770 and Qpid JMS 4 750: see <<one-at-a-time>>.

With a body of 10 kB, Tassis does 223 000 round trips a second in the JVM and 2 490 through the network (entry BENCH-20261013-01). Since milestone M12-5 the rows of the table that go through a Tassis server allocate less than it shows: 28 kB for the Tassis client, 18 kB for Qpid JMS.

== Several clients at once

Several threads, each with its connection, its session and its queue, each sending a message and receiving it. The figure is what they do between them.

[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
| Threads | Tassis, in the JVM | Artemis, in the JVM

| 1 | 3 372 000 | 6 800
| 2 | 1 906 000 | 14 800
| 4 | 1 540 000 | 26 800
| 8 | 1 528 000 | 53 500
| 16 | 1 463 000 | 94 100
|===

A Tassis broker has one lock, and it shows: more clients do not get more out of it, and the first of them alone gets more than twice what sixteen share. What it serves at worst is still fifteen times what Artemis does with sixteen threads, and five hundred times what one client of the network asks of it. The lock stays as it is.

[#one-at-a-time]
== One message at a time, or fetching ahead

Unless told otherwise, the Tassis client asks a broker of the network for a message when the application asks it for one. Qpid JMS has the broker send a thousand ahead, and so does the Tassis client with xref:usage.adoc#prefetch[`prefetch=1000`]. Emptying a queue of 10 000 messages from a `TassisServer` (entry BENCH-20261012-05):

[cols="2,1"]
|===
| Client | Time per message

| Tassis, one at a time | 183 µs
| Tassis, a thousand ahead | 6.3 µs
| Qpid JMS, one at a time | 192 µs
| Qpid JMS, a thousand ahead | 6.6 µs
|===

The two clients cost the same when they work the same way: the difference is the choice. A message sent and received shows it too: 2 790 times a second one at a time, 5 270 fetching ahead, 4 750 for Qpid JMS.

One at a time is the default because it keeps a message nobody asked for where another consumer can take it. Fetching ahead is for consumers that last and that work alone on their queue, or that all keep receiving: xref:usage.adoc#prefetch[Fetching ahead] says what it costs. It does nothing for a consumer opened and closed for each message, which is slower with it (1 090 round trips a second for 1 330), and nothing measurable for a listener.


== Delayed messages

A message sent with a delivery delay is kept aside until its time comes: a queue that holds many of them is nearly as fast as one that holds none (entry BENCH-20261012-06, measured in the evening, when the machine did everything some 10 % slower than for the figures above).

[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
| Delayed messages held by the queue | Round trips a second, in the JVM | Before milestone M12-4

| 0 | 2 783 000 | 3 104 000
| 1 000 | 2 490 000 | 169 000
| 10 000 | 2 249 000 | 9 800
|===

The first line is the same on both sides once the two are measured alternately: `BENCH.md` has the detail.

[#waiting]
== Consumers that wait

A consumer that waits in `receive`, or whose listener waits for its next message, costs the others nothing: it is woken by what concerns its queue or its subscription, and by nothing else.

[cols="1,1,1"]
|===
| Consumers waiting on other queues | Round trips a second, in the JVM | Before milestone M12-2

| 0 | 3 275 000 | 3 282 000
| 100 | 3 185 000 | 218 000
| 1 000 | 3 188 000 | 18 200
|===

The first column of figures is the entry BENCH-20261012-04. Until M12-2 every change of a broker woke every receive that waited on it, whatever the queue.

== Sessions and connections

A session costs next to nothing in the JVM: opening a session, a producer and a consumer for each message takes the round trip from 0.30 µs to 0.52 µs. Through the network it costs 0.4 ms, as much as the round trip itself (771 µs for 361 µs): keep a session open rather than open one for each message.

A connection of the network takes 1 ms to open and close, and 7.6 µs to borrow from a xref:usage.adoc#pool[pool] and give back. A connection of the JVM takes 0.9 µs to open and close, less than the pool takes (5.4 µs): the pool is for the network. That is why xref:usage.adoc#injection[injected contexts] come from a pool on the network only.

== Listeners

Messages delivered to a `MessageListener`, a hundred at a time:

[cols="2,1"]
|===
| Client and broker | Messages a second

| Tassis, in the JVM | 500 000
| Artemis, in the JVM | 132 000
| Tassis client, Tassis server | 6 100
| Qpid JMS, Tassis server | 37 900
|===

How the session acknowledges does not show in the JVM: 500 000, 491 000 and 518 000 for automatic, lazy and transacted. The Tassis client through the network is behind, fetching ahead or not (5 900 and 6 100, entry BENCH-20261012-05): the receiving is not what holds it back. That benchmark sends with the same client, whose sends wait for the broker where those of Qpid JMS do not; that is the likely cause, and it has not been established.

== What is still slow

* **Delivery to a listener through the network**, six times under Qpid JMS (see <<_listeners>>). The cause is not established.
* **A small message allocates three times what it does elsewhere**: 28 kB for a message of 100 bytes sent and received through a Tassis server, where Qpid JMS and Artemis allocate 9 kB between them. It was 38 kB before milestone M12-5, which took the copies of the body away: a message of 10 kB went from 553 kB to 195 kB (entry BENCH-20261013-01). What is left is the shape of a message, not its body.
* **Several clients of one broker of the JVM share its one lock** (see <<_several_clients_at_once>>), by decision.

== Running the benchmarks

From the root of the repository:

[source,bash]
----
# build the benchmarks, and check that each of them runs
./mvnw -ntp -Pbench -pl tassis-bench -am verify

# all of them: a little more than an hour
java -jar tassis-bench/target/benchmarks.jar -prof gc

# one of them, for one provider
java -jar tassis-bench/target/benchmarks.jar RoundTripBench -p provider=tassis-vm
----

`tassis-bench` is the only module of Tassis, with the interoperability tests, that depends on third-party code: JMH, Artemis and Qpid JMS. It is behind the Maven profile `bench`, is never published, and a plain build downloads nothing for it.

A figure that is published goes to `BENCH.md` first, with the machine, the command and the raw results.
